Mung bean and soybean: seed rates and soaking for 1020 trays

Reviewed November 6, 2026. Open the planner · Full source notebook.

Both crops are available in the lineup. Start with 150 g dry mung beans or 100 g dry soybeans per nominal 10 × 20 inch planted area. Weigh before soaking. These are pilot starting amounts selected from different kinds of evidence, not averages of interchangeable recipes or proven yield optima. Soybean has substantially weaker tray-scale evidence.

Crop Dry seed / nominal 1020 (200 in²) Mass density Presoak Selected calendar soak First harvest checks
Mung bean — Vigna radiata 150 g 0.75 g/in²; 1,162 g/m² 8–12 hours 10 hours Day 7–10 after sowing
Soybean — Glycine max 100 g pilot 0.50 g/in²; 775 g/m² 8 hours, supplier starting method 8 hours Day 7–14 after sowing

The mung mass is an editorial choice anchored to a primary density experiment [D11]; its soak comes from On The Grow [D62]. The soybean mass is a provisional trial informed by a small-pot study [D63] and supplier instructions with an unspecified tray [D64]. Fothergill’s independently describes soybean microgreens and a 7–14 day harvest window [D65]. None of these sources tests this complete coir/cover/calendar recipe.

Why 150 g for mung beans

Priti and colleagues tested 2, 3 and 4 seeds sown/cm² across 20 mung bean genotypes, alongside lentil, in 38 × 28 cm trays with coir, vermiculite and sand. They selected 3 seeds/cm² as a quality/yield compromise in their system. Higher density produced more stretched, crowded growth. This is a sown-seed count, so do not divide it by germination again. D11 — primary study

At 200 in² = 1,290.32 cm², that target is 3,871 seeds. The reported mung ten-seed weights of approximately 0.30–0.54 g imply about 116–209 g dry seed, depending on genotype. With the planner’s illustrative 25 seeds/g, the exact count benchmark is 154.84 g. The practical default is rounded to 150 g and stays a mass-based recipe; changing seeds/g changes its predicted stand, not the grams to weigh. Select Priti · 3 sown seeds/cm² benchmark to calculate grams from your actual lot count instead. [D11]

On The Grow explicitly recommends 8–12 hours of soaking for mung beans. The Priti paper does not specify a presoak, so the starter combines the density evidence with a separate grower soaking instruction. Its benchmark alternative retains the original documentation gap. D62 — grower FAQ

Published grower amounts vary markedly. Vitality Farms’ contributed notes list 450 g per 1020, also with an 8–12 h soak. That is three times this starting amount; its density is not independently optimized and its timing statements conflict. It corroborates the soak window but does not justify averaging the rates. Start lower, record stand quality and saleable harvest, then compare a modest higher rate using the same seed lot and conditions. D66 — grower notes, printed p. 40

Why soybean is labeled a pilot

Truzzi and colleagues grew soybean microgreens using 25 seeds in an 8 cm diameter pot, a 24-hour presoak, peat/expanded clay 80:20, 18°C and a final day-10 harvest. The 10 cm pots in the paper belong to peas, not soybean. There were five replicate pots per species/light/time combination; density was held constant. D63 — primary study, §2.1

The soybean sowing density is 25 ÷ (π × 4²) = 0.497 seeds/cm², equivalent to approximately 642 seeds per nominal 1020. The paper gives no soybean seed mass. At an illustrative 6.4 seeds/g, 642 seeds would weigh about 100 g; at 4 seeds/g they would weigh about 160 g. The illustrative count cannot establish a universal gram rate. Count and weigh a representative dry sample from the intended sprouting lot. [D63; conversion and seed-size examples are editorial arithmetic]

NutriPony’s indexed soybean instructions specify 8 hours soaking, 100 g per seedling tray, four blackout days and 7–9 days to harvest. They omit the tray dimensions, so the 100 g figure cannot be called a verified 1020 recommendation. The live page subsequently returned HTTP 410; this access limitation is preserved in the source notebook. The planner uses 100 g / 200 in² and 8 h as an explicitly provisional starting trial, not a reproduction of that supplier’s tray. D64 — supplier record

Fothergill’s identifies its seed as Glycine max, says overnight soaking for its sprouting method, and separately gives thin-sown tray instructions and 7–14 days for cut microgreens. It does not provide dry grams per 1020; its 50 g figure is the packet size. The 24 h soak in the study remains a separate research method, not the default or an upper limit to blend into an average. D65 — supplier instructions, SKU 3042

Applying these to your shallow tray

These reference densities use 200 in². If you choose the Bootstrap Farmer floor dimensions of 19.75 × 9.5 inches = 187.625 in² as your planted surface, the same densities give 140.7 g mung and 93.8 g soybean. The planner scales automatically with the entered area. Measure at the actual coir fill line; floor area and nominal tray size are different reference choices. D59 — tray dimensions

For either starter, weigh dry seed, soak in clean water, rinse, drain thoroughly and sow evenly on moist coir without heaps. The local calendar uses an empty contact cover with 0 lb added, checking around day 3–4 for mung and day 4 for soybean. These cover settings are editorial adaptations, not verified bean-specific paver loads. No extra stretch blackout is scheduled by default.

The light-readiness card retains the approximate 0.5–1 inch above-medium group cue and rooted, upright emergence check. This height is an editorial observation aid, not a measured prediction for either bean. If the stand is not ready, retain a loose, unweighted dark cover and recheck in 12 hours; stop extending darkness if stems lean or tangle. D41 — grower transition guidance

These are leafy, cut microgreen pilots, distinct from dark-grown bean sprouts eaten with seed and roots. Use untreated food-use seed and leave roots behind at harvest. Agronomic studies and soaking instructions do not validate raw-food safety or establish that soaking makes raw soy safe. See the existing mung/lentil primary paper for the separate legume evidence limitations. The two source recipes should be compared through local growing records rather than a predicted yield.
