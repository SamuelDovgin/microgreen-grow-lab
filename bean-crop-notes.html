Mung bean and soybean: seed rates and soaking for 1020 trays

Updated November 6, 2026 after the seed-rate recheck. Open the planner · Full source notebook.

The selected defaults are now 215 g dry mung beans and 150 g dry soybeans per nominal 10 × 20 inch / 200 in² planted surface. Weigh before soaking. Mung uses an explicit supplier tray rate; soybean is a starting trial within an area-scaled supplier range. These replace the earlier 150 g mung / 100 g soybean pilot defaults.

Crop Dry seed / nominal 1020 Density Presoak Selected calendar soak
Mung bean — Vigna radiata 215 g 1.075 g/in²; 1,666 g/m² 8–12 h 10 h
Soybean — Glycine max 150 g starting trial 0.750 g/in²; 1,162 g/m² 8 h within a supplier’s 8–12 h window 8 h

The starter calendars retain their coir/empty-cover adaptations and harvest checks around day 7–10 for mung and day 7–14 for soybean. Whole source recipes remain distinct. An established stand and desired leaf stage determine readiness; the date alone does not.

Mung: explicit tray recipe and a denser alternative

MP Seeds explicitly lists 215 g per 10 × 20 tray, approximately 10 seeds/g, 3–4 dark days and 8–9 total growing days. Its soak instructions conflict internally: the product table says up to 8 h, while its microgreen prose says 12 h. The starter therefore keeps the separately sourced 8–12 h window from On The Grow, with 10 h selected. Coir and the empty contact cover remain local adaptations. D67 — supplier product · D62 — grower FAQ

The supplied Vitality Farms guide lists 450 g per 1020, 8–12 h soak and a 3–4 day weighted stack. This is now selectable as Vitality Farms · 450 g grower recipe. Its added load has no numeric weight, so the planner displays 0 lb as an explicit placeholder rather than inventing a paver load. Its 7–10 day harvest statement conflicts with later wording about approximately seven days under lights. Its reported 19–21 oz yield is a grower observation, not a forecast for your tray. D66 — grower notes, printed p. 40

These 215 g and 450 g recipes are not averaged. Use the source option you intend to trial, record the seed lot, stand quality and saleable harvest, and compare rates under the same conditions.

Mung: retain the study count benchmark

Priti and colleagues tested 2, 3 and 4 seeds sown/cm² across 20 mung genotypes, in 38 × 28 cm trays with coir/vermiculite/sand. They selected 3 sown seeds/cm² as a quality/yield compromise; higher density produced stretched, crowded growth. That target equals 3,871 seeds per nominal 1020. It is a sown count, so germination is not divided into it again. D11 — primary study

The study’s ten-seed mung weights of about 0.30–0.54 g imply 116–209 g for that count. Those are different seed sizes from MP Seeds’ approximate 10 seeds/g. At 25 seeds/g the count target weighs 154.84 g; at 10 seeds/g it weighs 387.10 g. The Priti · 3 sown seeds/cm² benchmark option changes grams with your count input; the 215 g supplier starter keeps its mass fixed. Presoak was not specified by Priti; its benchmark’s zero-hour setting records that documentation gap.

Soybean: supplier range and separate dense-study evidence

Pestuj identifies Glycine max, approximately 5 seeds/g, 8–12 h soak and 162–216 g per 56 × 28 cm tray. Area-scaling those masses to 1,290.32 cm² gives 133.3–177.7 g per nominal 1020. The default 150 g is a practical selection inside that range, not an optimized rate. The supplier’s original method uses peat-based medium, 6–8 dark days and 12–16 total days; it is separately selectable as Pestuj · full supplier method, using the range midpoint of 189 g at its original area. The coir starter retains its own shorter readiness calendar. D68 — supplier instructions

A separate author manuscript by Castellaneta and colleagues uses 2 soybean seeds/cm² in 150 cm² peat trays, three initial dark days, nutrient solution and a day-10 cut. That corresponds to 2,581 seeds per nominal 1020; only if your lot has 5 seeds/g does the arithmetic become approximately 516 g. Density was fixed for a composition study, not optimized. This is evidence that much denser soybean protocols exist, not a tested 516 g commercial-tray recommendation or a reason to average unlike methods. D69 — primary paper · author manuscript, §2.2

The earlier Truzzi study used 25 seeds per 8 cm pot, approximately 0.497 seeds/cm², with a 24 h soak and day-10 endpoint. It reports no seed mass and does not establish an optimal tray density. NutriPony’s indexed 100 g per unspecified tray also lacks the footprint needed for a 1020 conversion. These remain historical comparison evidence rather than the default’s mass basis. D63 — primary study · D64 — supplier record; later returned HTTP 410

Fothergill’s separately gives thin-sown soybean microgreen instructions and 7–14 days to cut above seed; its 50 g figure is packet size. That harvest window remains part of the coir starter’s planning adaptation. D65 — supplier instructions, SKU 3042

These are independent crop recipes, not rates inferred from seed size alone. Seed count, cultivar, intended canopy density, growing medium and harvest stage affect the amount. The catalogue preserves each recipe and source trail.

What “Sanitize: Yes” means in the supplied guide

The mung row says Sanitize: Yes, but supplies no chemical, concentration, temperature, solution-to-seed ratio or contact time. Elsewhere the same guide says to sanitize by the grower’s preferred method and rinse. This indicates a seed treatment, distinct from the ordinary 8–12 hour hydration soak, but it is not an executable treatment recipe. The website does not invent a peroxide or bleach dose from that checkbox. [D66]

In practice, a defined seed treatment specifies a product suitable for the intended edible-seed use, its measured concentration, treatment conditions and duration, and whether a potable-water rinse is required. Follow that treatment and its product instructions, then use fresh clean water for the separate hydration soak. Washing and sanitizing trays is a separate equipment step; a surface-sanitizer dilution does not automatically apply to seed. FDA’s sprout guidance explains why treatment selection and chemical-label directions matter, and why surviving pathogens can multiply during germination. Its sprout-production guidance is not a universal microgreen treatment recipe. D70 — FDA guidance, seed treatment section

Applying these to your shallow tray

For a measured 19.75 × 9.5 inch = 187.625 in² planted surface, the selected reference densities become 201.7 g mung and 140.7 g soybean. The planner scales with the entered area. Measure at the coir fill line; the tray floor and nominal trade size are different reference choices. D59 — tray dimensions

Drain soaked seed and spread evenly without heaps. The starter uses an empty contact cover, with no added paver load. Its approximate 0.5–1 inch above-medium light-transition cue is an editorial group estimate, checked alongside rooted, upright emergence. If not ready, retain a loose unweighted dark cover and recheck; stop extending darkness if stems lean or tangle. D41 — grower transition criteria

These are leafy, cut microgreen recipes. Agronomic growth, a rinse or a soak does not establish raw-food safety. Source-specific count evidence and full per-field provenance remain available in the planner data and dated source notebook. The Truzzi primary PDF remains available offline.
