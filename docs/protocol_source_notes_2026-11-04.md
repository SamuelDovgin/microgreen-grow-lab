## Sunflower and broccoli

- **D05 and D04.** Piedmont Microgreens' 2024 table uses 120 g dry sunflower seed and an overnight soak. The 2025 table gives 130 g, three covered days in a stack and no top dress. It gives no paver mass or separate blackout duration. The planner's 120 g starting recipe uses the 2024 rate and soak, the three days from the 2025 table, and the paver and loose blackout from D31. The 9-hour midpoint represents the chosen overnight schedule. It is a planner choice within 6–12 h, since Piedmont does not give an exact duration. [2024 yield table](https://microgreenmanager.com/blog/microgreens-yield-cheatsheet) · [2025 germination table](https://microgreenmanager.com/blog/germination-id-guides)
- **D31.** On The Grow's 125 g sunflower guide describes a 15 lb paver over a solid contact tray for roughly four days. Once shoots lift the contact tray, remove the paver and flip the cover into a dark dome with room above the shoots for about one day. The current method rinses and sows without soaking. An eight-hour soak is a separate option, and the author reports more decay with longer soaks in their tests. In light the grower describes about one cup per bottom-watering check, once or twice daily. The planner keeps the two preparation options as separate recipes. Hull removal is a separate, targeted action. [Written guide](https://onthegrow.net/blogs/microgreens/how-to-grow-sunflower-microgreens) · [Earlier video demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kS_Zmu5E7VY)
- **V13 and D03.** Donny's demonstration uses a four-hour sunflower soak, 5 oz dry seed and a 14 lb paver. Utah State's household 1020 method lists a 12-hour sunflower soak with 48 g dry seed and gives a general cover example of 2–5 lb. The app uses 3 lb for the household example. These are distinct preparations. [Caption annotations](08_creator_transcript_density_and_water_review.md) · [Utah State Extension](https://extension.usu.edu/yardandgarden/research/grow-your-own-microgreens)
- **D32.** The broccoli guide reports similar results across no added weight, 3 lb and 15 lb in its tests. The grower inverts an empty contact tray so it gently touches the seeds, keeps it dark for 2–3 days and uses about one additional day with the tray lifted away from the seedlings. The app borrows that cover sequence for the broccoli starter while preserving the Johnny's seed-rate anchor. A separate coir recipe uses the grower's seed-rate range midpoint and earlier harvest window. The guide reports 1–2 cups/day under lights in its setup. [Broccoli guide](https://onthegrow.net/blogs/microgreens/how-to-grow-broccoli-microgreens) · [Johnny's trial](https://www.johnnyseeds.com/growers-library/vegetables/microgreens/micro-greens-yield-data-trial-summary-discussion.html)

## Height and uncovering readiness

Every crop now has an approximate height cue on its planned light-start card, plus an explicit instruction to keep it in blackout and recheck in 12 hours if it is not ready. Measure above the medium to the top of a typical seedling; a tall outlier or the tray rim is not the reference. Look for anchored roots and upright emergence across most of the tray. Height is an observation guide, not a mandatory minimum or a prediction of how tall a plant will be on a calendar date. Once uncovered, keep the crop under lights. If the transition is delayed, update the covered/blackout days so later tasks reflect the actual start.

| Broccoli | 0.5–1 in / 1.3–2.5 cm | D42 gives about 2 cm; D43 about 0.5 in. Range accommodates these method-dependent grower cues. |
| Other small brassicas; radish | 0.5–1 in / 1.3–2.5 cm | Editorial group estimate; no crop-specific numeric transition height verified. |
| Basil | 0.5–1 in / 1.3–2.5 cm | D44 grower cue. Clear-dome recipes retain their vented dome rather than adding darkness. |
| Chia; Apiaceae herbs; beet/chard; other legumes; alliums | 0.5–1 in / 1.3–2.5 cm | Editorial group estimate; use the crop's emergence/root cue. |

**D41** distinguishes pressure removal from loose blackout. Remove any load/contact tray once the stand lifts it or starts bending beneath it. Its method typically uses at most one extra dark day and ends blackout while shoots still support themselves; it does not establish a numeric height for every crop. The app preserves the selected recipe's dark-stage duration as a planning checkpoint. Do not keep weak or leaning seedlings dark merely to reach the height range. A clear humidity dome without a separate dark stage stays vented if the stand is not ready; lettuce and other light-sensitive recipes do not gain an imposed blackout phase.

## Water evidence and decisions

The daily target is a planning budget. At nominal 200 in², 2.0 mm/day is about 258 mL or 1.09 cups for broccoli and 3.7 mm/day is about 477 mL or 2.02 cups for sunflower late under lights. Those values are editable. Peas, wheatgrass and radish have higher planning defaults than most fine herbs. Sensitivity changes the displayed range. The late-to-early ramp is illustrative, and spray, initial coir hydration and soaking have separate purposes.

The higher-use defaults reflect grower context, not a controlled comparison of crop water loss. A Reddit grower describes sunflower, peas, radish and wheatgrass sometimes using twice their typical under-light amount. Bootstrap Farmer describes bottom watering and checking the medium after absorption. Replace the estimate with observations from your trays as soon as available.
