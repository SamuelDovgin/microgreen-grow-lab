# Clean and sanitize your trays

*Updated November 12, 2026 · reusable empty trays between crops.*

**Yes: clean and sanitize after every finished tray/crop cycle, before planting again.** Include the perforated grow tray, solid watering tray, blackout/weighting tray and reusable covers. This is a between-crops reset, not a reason to spray sanitizer onto a growing crop. On The Grow recommends sanitation between grows; Virginia Cooperative Extension likewise recommends cleaning growing containers between rotations. [T04,T06]

Start with the workflow below, then choose one method. **Bleach is an option. Whether you rinse afterward depends on the exact product and the labeled use.** Use the food-contact sanitizing directions for routine empty-tray sanitation, and check that the product and tray manufacturer allow the application. [T01,T02,T03]

## Can I buy Dollar Tree or store-brand bleach?

**Yes, if the exact bottle meets the checklist below. Clorox branding and a 121 fl oz bottle are not requirements.** A store-brand product can be suitable; price and store name do not establish its permitted use. Choose a regular unscented liquid chlorine bleach with a matching EPA-registered label that permits the food-contact sanitizing application. [T02,T19,T21,T22; selection synthesis]

**A small-bottle option:** Dollar Tree's same-day catalog lists [Clorox Disinfecting Bleach, Concentrated Formula, Regular — 11 fl oz](https://sameday.dollartree.com/store/dollar-tree/products/20971706-clorox-disinfecting-bleach-concentrated-formula-regular-11-fl-oz). This is a specific item to look for, subject to your store's stock. Check the bottle in your hand against the checklist; a retailer listing is not a substitute for its directions. [T19]

### What is actually necessary on the label?

1. **The right product type:** regular, unscented **liquid chlorine bleach**, with **sodium hypochlorite** identified as its active ingredient and its percentage stated. For this guide's shopping route, skip fragranced, thickened/splashless, gel, outdoor, color-safe/non-chlorine and cleaner-with-bleach products. A generic cleaning product containing bleach is not the same bottle as a labeled food-contact sanitizer. [T02,T17; selection rule]
2. **An EPA registration number:** look for **“EPA Reg. No.”** on a US product. **“EPA Est. No.” alone is not enough**; it identifies a production establishment and does not establish the product's antimicrobial registration. [T21]
3. **The intended use:** the bottle or its exact matching official label must permit **sanitizing hard, nonporous food-contact surfaces**—for example, dishes, utensils or food-handling equipment. A laundry-whitening claim, “kills germs” headline, SDS, or bathroom-disinfecting instruction alone does not supply this use. Check material compatibility with your plastic tray as well. [T01,T03,T21,T22]
4. **A complete set of directions:** find the **bleach amount + water amount, application method, full wet contact time, final rinse/no-rinse instruction, and draining/drying directions** for that same food-contact use. Follow any water-temperature, concentration-testing, protective-equipment and other conditions stated there. Do not borrow the dose from another brand or combine directions from different uses. [T01,T02,T22]
5. **A usable bottle and solution:** keep the product in its original readable container, follow its storage/date guidance and make fresh diluted bleach daily. Verify the working solution with appropriate **chlorine sanitizer test strips** when its label specifies a concentration range; choose strips covering that range, rather than quat or unrelated pool tests. A bleach smell is not a measurement. [T02; practical measurement guidance]

**There is no single stock percentage that makes every brand interchangeable.** CDC's general household-disinfection advice discusses 5–9% bleach, but that is not a universal food-contact buying specification. A different-strength product needs its own authorized directions; neither a high percentage nor an unscented label alone establishes suitability. Do not apply the Clorox teaspoon recipe to an unidentified Dollar Tree bleach. [T02,T21,T22]

### What about LA's Totally Awesome bleach?

Check the exact variant. The manufacturer's **Bleach Fresh Scent** page explicitly describes floral fragrance; that is outside this guide's unscented selection. This research did not establish a complete matching food-contact sanitizing label for an unscented LA's Totally Awesome bottle. Its brand name alone is therefore not a recommendation or a rejection of every product it makes. [T20]

**In the aisle:** if the bottle lacks a readable active percentage, EPA registration or matching food-contact directions, choose a different product for this tray-sanitation routine. If the directions require a rinse, finish the contact time and rinse with potable water; if they specify no rinse, drain and air-dry as directed. A stronger mix does not fix missing directions. [T01,T02,T21,T22; selection synthesis]

## Every crop cycle: the six steps

1. **Empty.** After harvest, remove roots, hulls and spent coir/media. Handle waste away from clean trays and food. Use fresh medium for the next tray in this home workflow.
2. **Wash.** Remove loose debris with water, then scrub compatible plastic trays with dish detergent and warm water. Work into drainage holes, corners, ribs, rims and undersides with a dedicated nonabrasive brush. Inspect for cracks or rough damage that traps residue; replace trays that cannot be cleaned.
3. **Rinse the soap off.** Use potable water. Sanitizer goes onto the cleaned, rinsed tray in a separate clean basin. Soap and dirt left behind can interfere with the treatment.
4. **Sanitize with one compatible product.** Follow its food-contact directions for dilution, water conditions and application. Wet every surface; separate nested trays and remove trapped air. Begin timing once the whole surface is wet, and maintain wet contact for the entire specified time.
5. **Rinse only when that use requires it.** Complete the contact time first. Use potable water when the label requires a final rinse; a labeled no-rinse sanitizing application normally proceeds straight to draining.
6. **Air-dry and protect.** Drain on a clean rack. Let trays dry completely before nesting or storage; keep them off the floor and away from dirty wash splash. Avoid towel-drying or handling the cleaned interior. Reclean if a tray is contaminated during storage. [T01–T06; adapted operating sequence]

**Finding:** growers and extension sources agree on removing organic matter, cleaning and preventing recontamination. No sanitizer compensates for roots or biofilm left in the holes. The sequence reduces cross-contamination; it does not sterilize the tray or guarantee a pathogen-free crop. [T03–T06]

## Bleach step by step

**A concrete example:** Clorox's current US instructions for **Clorox Disinfecting Bleach** use **2 US teaspoons in 1 US gallon of cool potable water**, followed by **2 minutes submerged**, then **drain and air-dry without a final water rinse**. This is its food-contact sanitizing example. It is conditional on the directions on your bottle matching and the tray material being compatible. [T01]

| Starting water | Bleach for this specific example | Wet contact | Afterward |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 US gallon / 3.785 L | 2 US tsp / about 9.9 mL | 2 minutes | Drain; air-dry; no water rinse |
| 2 US gallons / 7.571 L | 4 US tsp / about 19.7 mL | 2 minutes | Same |

The second row scales the same published recipe; mL values are unit conversions. **These are bleach volumes, not grams.** Pick a basin that fits a 1020 tray and measure the actual water volume; a tote's nominal capacity is not the amount you filled it with. [T01; calculated]

1. Check the bottle's exact identity, strength, expiration/storage guidance and food-contact instructions. Choose a plain, unscented product with the appropriate directions; scented, splashless, gel and laundry-only formulations cannot be substituted from this recipe. If the directions differ, use that product's label rather than this example. [T01,T02]
2. Wash and rinse the trays first. In a separate clean compatible basin, measure cool potable water, then add the measured bleach. Keep detergent out of this basin. [T01]
3. Wearing the label-specified protection and working with ventilation, fully wet both faces, rims and holes. Do not tightly nest trays during treatment. For the matching Clorox example, submerge for the full 2 minutes. [T01,T02; application adaptation]
4. Drain onto a clean drying rack. Use the final-rinse instruction for the specific labeled application; the example above needs no additional water rinse. Let trays dry before storage. [T01]
5. Prepare fresh diluted bleach daily, and replace sooner if dirty or outside the labeled concentration. Use chlorine test strips matched to the label's range and follow their instructions; odor and solution clarity cannot verify sanitizer strength. Do not top up an unknown dirty bath by guessing. [T02,T03; operating synthesis]

**Never mix bleach with vinegar/acids, ammonia, peroxide, other disinfectants or detergent.** Wash and rinse as separate steps. Keep chemicals away from seeds, greens, fertilizer mixing and children/pets. Follow label storage, protective-equipment and disposal instructions; do not pour spent sanitizer into growing trays or a nutrient reservoir. [T02; operating synthesis]

### Greenhouse rinse-off method

**For an empty, hard plastic 1020 tray, keep it visibly wet for 10 minutes, rinse thoroughly with clean water, then air-dry.** UC IPM's greenhouse guidance uses about **0.5% sodium hypochlorite** to disinfect clean, nonporous equipment and recommends a clean-water rinse to avoid seedling injury. Check that your bleach label and tray manufacturer permit the use and application method. [UC IPM greenhouse guidance](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/floriculture-and-ornamental-nurseries/management-of-soilborne-pathogens/)

1. Scrub out old media and roots, then rinse away the detergent.
2. For **500 mL finished**, use **33.33 mL of regular, unscented 7.5% liquid bleach + 466.67 mL water**. This is an approximate dilution by volume of 1 part bleach to 14 parts water. Confirm the 7.5% strength and directions on your Tandil jug. Aldi lists a 121 oz 7.5% product. [Aldi product listing](https://www.aldi.us/store/aldi/products/128146330-tandil-75-percent-bleach-121-oz)
3. Wet the entire tray by a method allowed on the bottle and keep it wet for the full 10 minutes. Rinse the corners and drain holes as well as the flat surfaces, then air-dry before adding soil or coir.

The greenhouse method targets contamination on reusable growing containers and includes a water rinse to remove residual bleach. The 150 ppm food-contact soak in the EPA master label has its own two-minute contact time and drain-only finish. Use the dilution, wet time and finish from the same method. [EPA 7.5% bleach master label](https://www3.epa.gov/pesticides/chem_search/ppls/055852-00006-20260206.pdf) · [UC IPM greenhouse guidance](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/floriculture-and-ornamental-nurseries/management-of-soilborne-pathogens/)

### Why a grower's “10% bleach” recipe is different

One part stock bleach plus nine parts water is a tenfold dilution of that stock, not a universal food-contact recipe. For example, 5% stock becomes approximately 0.5% / 5,000 ppm sodium hypochlorite. It is very different from a teaspoon-scale gallon recipe. Stock strength and concentration units matter; this arithmetic is an explanation, not a treatment instruction. [T04; calculated]

Do not combine a strong disinfecting dilution from one guide with a no-rinse claim or short contact time from another. Follow one complete product/use instruction. [T01–T04]

## Choose your method

Always wash and rinse first. The following examples apply to empty, compatible, hard, nonporous surfaces, not seeds, edible greens or irrigation water.

| Method | Exact example / how to apply | Wet contact | Final water rinse? | Practical fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Bleach** | Matching Clorox food-contact example: 2 US tsp per US gallon, fully submerge | 2 minutes | **No** for this example; drain and air-dry | Inexpensive home option; check bottle directions and chlorine strength |
| **Peracetic acid + peroxide** | SaniDate **5.0**, specimen label V22.2: 1.6–5.4 US fl oz per 5 US gallons potable water (147–500 ppm PAA); thoroughly wet the precleaned surface | 1 minute | **No** for this food-contact sanitizing use; drain thoroughly | Commercial alternative; concentrate needs label-specified protection and accurate measurement |
| **Quat tablets** | Steramine **1-G, 1.5 g tablet** example: 1 tablet per US gallon warm water gives 200 ppm; fully dissolve, then immerse or apply as labeled | At least 1 minute; governing code may require longer | No rinse/wipe in the cited surface-sanitizing directions; drain and air-dry | Convenient dosing; verify with quat strips, not chlorine strips; check water conditions |
| **Hydrogen peroxide product** | Use a product whose label actually permits the food-contact surface/use; follow its own rate and application | Product-specific | Product-specific | Grower-used option; “3%” or “food grade” alone does not establish approved contact time or a no-rinse use |
| **Heat / dishwasher** | Use only if the tray manufacturer permits the temperature/cycle and the sanitizing process is validated for that item | Equipment-specific | No chemical rinse issue | Not the default for plastic 1020 trays; ordinary washing is not proof of heat sanitation |

[T01,T03–T05,T07,T08]

**SaniDate details:** use the food-contact sanitizing section, not a different rate from the same label. The cited product also has disinfecting applications that require a potable-water rinse on food-contact surfaces. Never transfer this recipe to SaniDate 12.0, ZeroTol or another formulation. [T07]

**Steramine details:** the cited EPA label is dated June 12, 2014; the manufacturer's live product page still describes 1-G tablets at one per gallon. Check the current container label before use, including water hardness/temperature limits. The 1-QT tablet is a different tablet size and dose. The archived label does not establish that every current package or locally approved use is identical. [T08,T09]

**Peroxide details:** On The Grow describes a 3% peroxide method, but the retrieved grower text is not an exact product label. This guide therefore does not turn any pharmacy peroxide bottle into a verified food-contact sanitizing recipe. If no applicable label is available, choose one of the documented options instead. [T04]

**Heat details:** Bootstrap Farmer allows dishwasher washing of its heavy-duty trays with heated drying turned off. On The Grow cautions against high-heat sanitizing cycles for plastic, and its own current reusable-medium FAQ prohibits dishwasher use for its medium. Check the exact item: silicone medium care does not establish plastic-tray heat tolerance. [T03–T05]

## Do I rinse afterward?

**Before sanitizer: yes—rinse away detergent. After sanitizer: read the directions for that exact use.** [T01,T07,T08]

- **Matching labeled no-rinse food-contact sanitizer:** finish the required wet contact, drain and air-dry as directed. No extra rinse is needed for the examples above.
- **Label says rinse food-contact surfaces after disinfection:** finish the contact time, rinse with potable water, then air-dry. Do not rinse early.
- **Unknown concentration, mixed-up recipe or missing food-contact directions:** do not assume air-drying or a quick rinse fixes it. Stop using that batch; follow the label's disposal instructions, rewash/rinse the trays and start with a known compatible labeled method. [T01,T02,T07,T08; corrective-action synthesis]

No-rinse permission applies to a specified product, dilution, surface and use. It does not mean that bleach concentrate or a stronger disinfecting mixture can be left on a tray.

## How often, and what counts as a tray?

- **After each crop cycle, before reuse:** wash/sanitize the grow tray, reservoir/catch tray, covers and reusable contact equipment. A clean-looking tray still gets the reset. [T04,T06]
- **During the same healthy crop:** this guide does not call for bleaching a planted tray after each watering. Remove stagnant excess water, keep hands/tools clean and monitor slime, odor and disease. Surface-sanitation recipes are not crop treatments. [T06; practical scope]
- **New or stored trays:** wash before first food-growing use and sanitize according to the compatible label. Protect cleaned trays during storage; if dusty, dirty or exposed to pests/splash, repeat the cleaning/sanitation sequence. [T03,T06; adaptation]
- **Failed or visibly contaminated crop:** isolate and discard the suspect crop/media; clean the affected trays and nearby contact surfaces before reuse. Review seed, water, wetness and airflow. A routine sanitizing claim does not prove elimination of every plant pathogen or make a suspect crop edible. [T04,T10; project precaution]
- **Harvest tools:** clean and sanitize before harvest and after contamination; complete the required contact time. A quick dip between trays does not automatically meet a labeled treatment. [T08,T10]
