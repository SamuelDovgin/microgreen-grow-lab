You do not need expensive, specialized grow lights to grow microgreens. Affordable white LED shop lights, including the T5 models you're looking at on Amazon, can work very well.

For standard 1020 trays (10 × 20 inches), I'd start with approximately 15–25 watts of actual LED power per tray, assuming reasonably efficient lights mounted close to the plants. But the more important measurement is how much light actually reaches the seedlings.

1. What does T5 mean?

T5 describes the tube size, not how powerful the light is.

T = tubular

5 = five eighths of an inch in diameter (approximately 16 mm)

T8 = eight eighths, or 1 inch (25.4 mm)

Originally, these labels described fluorescent tubes. LED manufacturers now also use them for similarly shaped fixtures.

A T5 light isn't automatically better or worse than a T8, and T5 does not mean full-spectrum or plant-specific lighting.

For microgreens, focus on the light's intensity, efficiency, coverage, color spectrum, and price instead.

2. How much light does a microgreen tray actually need?
The scientific metric is PPFD, measured in µmol/m²/s. It tells you how much photosynthetically useful light reaches the plants.

Virginia Tech's 2026 microgreen production guide recommends a daily light integral (DLI) of approximately 9–16 mol/m²/day, depending on the crop and production goals.
